As shown in ~\Cref{fig:gate_embedding}, MiniLM embeddings form multiple local clusters within the same intent category. That means intent embeddings are not globally compact but locally concentrated. As a result, hard OOS queries can sit close to known-intent regions, and a single boundary cannot capture this overlap. To handle this, we model each intent category with multiple local clusters. As shown in ~\Cref{fig:gate_score_distribution}, this design works: most known-intent samples fall inside the cluster boundaries, while most OOS samples fall outside. That means local geometry separates the two regions effectively. However, the overlap near the threshold comes from ambiguous utterances at cluster edges, which indicates that gate decisions depend on local semantic structure.
